Professional narrative

I am a board-certified internal medicine physician and acute care hospitalist. Texas shaped the whole arc — biomedical engineering and medical school at Texas A&M, internal medicine residency at Texas Health Presbyterian in Dallas, then two years as an assistant professor in the Hospitalist Division of McGovern Medical School, practicing at Memorial Hermann Hospital–Texas Medical Center. In the spring of 2026 my fiancée and I moved to Philadelphia for her doctoral training in clinical psychology, and I am planting professional roots of my own here.

My foundation is high-volume, high-acuity inpatient medicine: a group carrying more than 40% of the institution’s admissions, censuses above twenty medically and sociomedically complex patients, rapid responses and code events, goals-of-care facilitation, and continuous coordination across medical, surgical, and allied teams. I staffed admissions with internal medicine residents and revised a triage primer for each incoming intern class. Written commendations followed for documentation compliance and intra-departmental relations.

Scope past the bedside is the habit I inherited from that group: triage shared with the emergency department, intensive care units, and transfer center; root-cause analysis for the division’s quality assurance committee; Epic SlicerDicer credentialing in support of capacity management analytics; and serial early-user testing across three separate AI implementations. My engineering background carries the rest — Python, SQL, and Bash built up largely after hours into the projects listed alongside this, which I build and maintain myself. Two convictions run through all of it: modern physicians deserve modern tools, and those tools must never compromise information sovereignty.

Projects

Clinical and peri-clinical data work. Two repositories are public and carry their own full write-ups, methods, and provenance notes; the rest are in private development and are described here in outline.

Public repositories

hupsim

v1 shipped

A room-level capacity model of a single academic hospital campus, built entirely from cited public sources with a confidence tag on every structural fact and all dynamics explicitly simulated.

  • simulation
  • capacity modeling
  • geospatial
  • provenance

mimicwarehouse

In progress

A local EMR data warehouse over MIMIC-IV with end-to-end provenance, protocol-frozen inquiry over retrospective data, and a pre-commit leak guard — data never leaves the machine. Public while in flight; v1 is still some way off.

  • MIMIC-IV
  • DuckDB
  • Polars
  • Streamlit
  • data governance

Private & in development

Described in outline while the work matures. Methods, code structure, and results are available to discuss directly, and each repository opens publicly once it reaches a defensible state.

phillysim

Private

Municipal disparity modeling for Philadelphia — linking public administrative and health data to make neighborhood-level inequity legible at the scale decisions are actually made.

wikemgapsim

Private

Modeling editorial coverage gaps in WikEM, the open emergency medicine reference — where the clinical knowledge commons is thin, and whom that thinness reaches.
